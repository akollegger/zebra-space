A four-way stop intersection has arms North, South, East, and West, plus a pedestrian crosswalk
on the eastern approach. A pedestrian and one car from each direction all want to go through.
None of the four cars are turning — each one drives straight through the intersection.

General rules:

1. A pedestrian using a crosswalk always goes before a car approaching from the direction that
   crosswalk crosses.
2. Otherwise, cars (and the pedestrian) go in the order they arrive at the intersection.
3. If two cars arrive at the same moment, right-of-way rotates clockwise starting from North —
   of the tied cars, whichever comes first in the order North, East, South, West goes first.

This morning:

4. The pedestrian is crossing at the eastern crosswalk.
5. The pedestrian and the East car arrive at the same moment.
6. The South car arrives before the pedestrian and the East car.
7. The North car and the West car arrive at the same moment, after the pedestrian and the East
   car.

In what order do the pedestrian and the four cars go through the intersection?
